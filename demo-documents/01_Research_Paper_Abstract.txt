TITLE:   ProofChain: A Hybrid IPFS–Blockchain Architecture for
         Cryptographically Verifiable Document Existence Proofs

AUTHORS: Khushi Jangid, Kshitiz Yadav, Kunal Upadhyay
         Department of Computer Science & Engineering
         Engineering College, Ajmer (Autonomous Institution, Govt. of Rajasthan)

DATE:    September 2026
VERSION: 1.0 (Original Submission)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Centralized document storage platforms (e.g., Google Drive, Dropbox) provide
convenience-level version history that is entirely controlled by the hosting
platform. A third party verifying a document's authenticity via such services
must unconditionally trust that platform's internal logs — an inherently
unauditable process.

This paper presents ProofChain, a system that combines content-addressed
distributed storage (IPFS) with Ethereum smart-contract timestamping to provide
portable, third-party-verifiable proof of document existence. Uploaded documents
are pinned to IPFS via Pinata to produce a Content Identifier (CID) — a
cryptographic hash of the document content. The CID is then anchored on a
Solidity smart contract along with the uploader's Ethereum address and the
block timestamp, creating an immutable, on-chain record.

A MongoDB metadata layer enables fast search by filename and uploader address,
separating the mutable convenience layer from the immutable on-chain proof.
A React/Ethers.js frontend provides a three-panel interface: Upload, History,
and Verify — the last of which allows any party to independently confirm a
document's authorship and timestamp without trusting the ProofChain platform
itself.

Evaluation demonstrates: (a) successful IPFS pin reliability across two
geographic regions (FRA1, NYC1); (b) constant-cost Solidity anchor transactions
averaging 47,832 gas units; and (c) sub-second verification latency for
already-pinned CIDs.
